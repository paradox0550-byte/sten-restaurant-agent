# Guest Service, Sales and Service Recovery

## Source basis

Primary internal source: *Стандарт качества обслуживания гостей*, 2025, author A.S. Dumrauf. The document states that it was prepared for restaurants «Азиатский квартал» and «#Farш» and includes a confidentiality notice. Treat it as internal, venue-specific source material; do not redistribute it as a public generic standard.

A separate supplied file titled *Искусство сервиса в ресторане Casual Dining* contains only a cover and a short outline in the uploaded copy. Its outline covers guest expectations, restaurant image, appearance, service, difficult situations, waiter psychology and operational management. It is not a full text source and must not be used to invent chapter content.

## 1. Service objective

The 2025 standard describes service as a sequence of professional interactions that shape guest experience and the restaurant's image. Its stated intent is to make the guest want to return. Kitchen quality and service quality both matter; neither should be assumed to compensate for a serious failure in the other.

The standard emphasizes attention, courtesy, tact, hospitality, knowledge of the restaurant and the ability to handle non-standard situations.

## 2. Preparation before service

The internal standard says a waiter should arrive 15–20 minutes before the shift in that restaurant's historical procedure. It also expects:
- clean, pressed uniform and appropriate appearance;
- knowledge of the current stop-list;
- table setting and dining-room preparation;
- clean furniture and guest-facing surfaces;
- readiness to take and enter orders in the restaurant system;
- readiness to serve, clear and reset tables;
- responsibility for guest service quality and support for less experienced waiters.

The arrival time is source-specific and must not be adopted as a universal labor rule.

## 3. Sales and guest-service sequence

The internal standard presents sales as a sequence:
1. establish contact — meet and greet;
2. identify guest needs;
3. present menu items;
4. answer questions and handle objections;
5. complete the primary sale;
6. offer an additional order;
7. settle the bill and complete the interaction.

The source says guest-facing sales are not only the waiter's responsibility; host/hostess, bartender and manager may also influence the guest's choice.

### Meeting and greeting
- A warm and timely welcome creates the first impression.
- The manager usually leads seating in the source's setting, but a waiter should help when the manager is occupied.
- The source expects the team to notice arriving guests rather than leave them unattended.
- Do not turn a historical script into a rigid phrase that sounds unnatural.

### Identifying needs and presenting the menu
- Observe whether the guest is ready to order; do not interrupt needlessly.
- Ask clarifying questions to understand taste, appetite, dietary restrictions and timing.
- Explain dishes and drinks only from reliable menu/recipe information.
- Describe taste, portion, ingredients and preparation accurately.
- Offer relevant additions; do not push irrelevant or misleading upsells.
- Do not guess ingredients, allergens, availability or preparation time.

### Order taking and service
- Enter the order accurately into the approved system.
- Confirm material details and modifiers.
- Coordinate with kitchen and bar.
- Keep the guest informed if timing changes.
- Deliver correct dishes and drinks to the correct guests.
- Maintain table cleanliness and replace used items appropriately.
- Do not remove an item a guest is still using without checking.

### Bill, payment and farewell
- Present and explain the bill accurately.
- Use approved cash and payment procedures.
- Service does not end at payment: remain attentive while guests finish.
- Thank guests and say goodbye warmly.
- Invite a return naturally and share upcoming events only when information is current and approved.

## 4. Communication and etiquette

The internal standard emphasizes respectful address, appropriate distance, attentive non-verbal communication and avoiding overfamiliarity. It specifically discourages addressing a guest as «девушка» or «мужчина» and recommends learning a guest's name when natural and appropriate.

Use this as a communication principle, not as a universal demand to ask every guest for a name. Respect the guest's personal space and preferred interaction level.

## 5. Questions, objections and complaints

The source describes listening, understanding the actual concern, acknowledging the guest's experience and proposing a way to resolve the issue. It provides example apology and resolution phrases, but the exact remedy must remain within the employee's delegated authority.

Operational sequence:
1. Listen without interrupting.
2. Clarify the specific problem.
3. Acknowledge the guest's experience.
4. Apologize when the restaurant has failed.
5. Explain only verified facts.
6. Offer remedies within authority.
7. Bring in the manager when the issue exceeds authority or remains unresolved.
8. Confirm the agreed solution and timing.
9. Follow up and record the outcome.

The source identifies aggression, abusive language and threats as escalation triggers. A waiter should not argue with or attempt to prove the guest wrong. Threats or risk to safety require immediate escalation under the restaurant's security/emergency procedure.

## 6. Service recovery data

For each material complaint, distinguish:
- guest-reported statement;
- staff observation;
- POS/KDS or other system evidence;
- confirmed failure, if established;
- immediate containment;
- agreed remedy;
- authorized decision-maker;
- responsible role and timing;
- verification of completion;
- recurrence-prevention hypothesis.

A complaint is not resolved merely because a discount, replacement or apology was offered. Completion and verification are separate states.

## 7. Guest experience measurement

Where reliable data exist, monitor:
- acknowledgement time;
- order accuracy;
- quoted versus actual waiting time;
- complaint incidence and recurrence;
- recovery time;
- guest feedback;
- accessibility barriers;
- repeat visits where lawfully and reliably measured;
- workload and staffing context.

Every metric requires a defined source, denominator, period and scope. Do not optimize speed at the expense of safety, accuracy or dignity.
