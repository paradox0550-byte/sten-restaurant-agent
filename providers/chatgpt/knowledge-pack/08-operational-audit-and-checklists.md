# Operational Audit, Checklists, Records and Verification

## 1. Audit workflow

1. Define establishment, outlet, zone, shift, date and scope.
2. Identify the applicable approved standard and its version.
3. Collect observations and evidence; distinguish observation from hearsay.
4. Classify severity only with approved local criteria.
5. Assign a responsible role, deadline and corrective action.
6. Verify completion with evidence; a checked box or verbal assurance alone may be insufficient.
7. Record recurrence and unresolved risks.
8. Escalate immediate food, guest, staff, fire or security risks through the approved local procedure.

## 2. Finding structure

A useful finding contains:
- finding ID;
- establishment/outlet/zone;
- date/time and observer;
- exact observed condition;
- source/evidence reference;
- applicable standard and version;
- evidence class (law, approved local standard, source-specific practice, proposal or unknown);
- impact/risk;
- severity and reason;
- immediate containment;
- corrective action;
- owner role and due date;
- verification method/evidence;
- status and recurrence link.

Do not invent an owner, due date, risk score or legal requirement.

## 3. Severity

Use a local approved severity model. The repository's Stage 11.1 workplan proposes:
- Critical — potential unsafe food handling, material legal misstatement, incorrect payroll/financial result, privacy breach or unsupported instruction likely to cause harm.
- High — material process gap, incorrect responsibility/authority, missing control or misleading applicability.
- Medium — incomplete handoff, record, exception or verification detail impairing repeatability.
- Low — terminology/discoverability/editorial inconsistency without material decision impact.
- Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to rate.

Severity is a review-priority label, not a legal risk score. The establishment must approve its actual escalation thresholds.

## 4. Checklists and records

A checklist should specify:
- what is checked;
- when and by whom, if the source or approved policy defines it;
- evidence expected;
- acceptable result and source of that criterion;
- deviation response;
- correction owner;
- verification and retention rules.

Do not turn a template into a legal requirement. Do not mark a check as passed when the source report was not supplied; use not checked / no data / not applicable.

## 5. Shift handover

Capture:
- what happened;
- what was verified;
- what remains open;
- current stop-list/availability issues;
- unresolved guest cases;
- equipment/maintenance issues;
- food-safety deviations and containment;
- staffing/coverage issues;
- stock and delivery exceptions;
- assigned actions, owner role and deadline;
- next-shift information.

A handover is not complete merely because a message was sent. The receiving role should acknowledge critical items.

## 6. Corrective-action lifecycle

Use distinct states:
- observed;
- assessed;
- action proposed;
- approved;
- in progress;
- completed_unverified;
- verified;
- closed;
- reopened.

A checked box, verbal assurance or generated response is not automatically proof of correction. Preserve the evidence trail and reopen recurring failures.

## 7. Autonomy boundaries

- Observer: records findings without messages or tasks.
- Copilot: prepares recommendations and drafts actions for manager approval.
- Operator: only pre-authorized low-risk actions with audit log and clear rollback.

Personnel decisions, pay/schedule changes, purchases/payments, price/menu/recipe/SOP changes and final closure of critical food-safety incidents remain with authorized humans unless a specific approved control grants a narrower action.

## Stage 11 professional practice expansion — integrated restaurant audit

**Provenance:** anonymized practical materials S11-08 (Service Design), S11-11 (internal audit), S11-03 (fire-readiness instruction), S11-04 (short training), S11-06 (dish quality).

### Evidence-based walkthrough
Structure the check by guest journey, employee readiness, POS order handling, pass and beverage quality, production flow, stock/receiving, food-safety records, equipment, emergency readiness and handover. Every observation needs location/shift/time, expected local standard, actual evidence, severity rationale, accountable role, containment, corrective action and verification date.

### Action versus completion
A manager can immediately contain a defective dish, blocked flow or missing label; root-cause work and preventive measures require follow-up. Separate “task assigned”, “action performed” and “effectiveness verified”. Repeated findings are management signals, not automatically employee misconduct.

### Training from audit findings
Convert repeated issues into targeted microtraining, demonstration and follow-up observation. Measure whether the behavior changed. For fire and food-safety issues, historical source documents are practical prompts only; qualified personnel must confirm current object-specific requirements.
