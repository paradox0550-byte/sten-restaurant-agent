# Food Safety, Product Adjacency, Marking and Cleaning Inventory

## Operating model and applicability

Food safety controls must be designed around the actual menu, ingredients, process flow, equipment, premises, suppliers, consumer profile and applicable jurisdiction. The following is a professional operating framework for identifying contamination pathways and designing local controls. It is not a legal register or a substitute for a site-specific hazard analysis.

## 1. Product separation and storage compatibility

Storage compatibility is the controlled placement of products to prevent biological, chemical, physical and allergen cross-contact, deterioration and transfer of unwanted odours.

It recommends separating:
- dry products (flour, sugar, grains, pasta and similar);
- bread;
- meat;
- fish;
- dairy/fat products;
- gastronomic products;
- vegetables and fruit;
- raw and heat-treated vegetables;
- rye and wheat bread.

Treat the following as risk combinations requiring separation or validated controls:
1. odor-emitting products (for example, herring and spices) with odor-absorbing products (tea, salt, sugar);
2. raw products/semi-finished products with ready-to-eat food;
3. frozen products with chilled products where temperature regimes differ;
4. dry/loose products with wet products;
5. food products with non-food products.

Where equipment is shared, define a documented segregation plan using dedicated zones or shelves, closed identified containers, vertical separation where relevant, and a cleaning/handling sequence that prevents cross-contact. Never assume that a venue-size threshold waives safe segregation; verify any claimed exception against current binding rules before use.

## 2. Identification and traceability controls

### Incoming raw materials
Receiving controls should verify the information required for the specific product and applicable technical rules, including on transport/consumer packaging including:
- product name;
- composition;
- quantity;
- manufacture date;
- shelf life;
- storage conditions;
- manufacturer name and location;
- recommendations/restrictions for use;
- nutritional values;
- GMO-related information;
- single market circulation mark.

The exact mandatory fields depend on product type and current technical regulation. Do not tell a restaurant to create missing package data itself; verify supplier documentation and applicable rules.

### In-house preparations
Each in-house preparation container should carry the fields required by the approved product-specific control system, normally including:
- product name;
- manufacture date and time;
- date and time until which it may be used.

Do not apply one label template to every product. The responsible specialist must validate shelf-life, time/temperature and frozen-product labelling rules for each product group.

### Cutting boards and knives
The establishment must implement a consistent identification system for boards, knives and other food-contact tools. It must distinguish at least the relevant raw/ready-to-eat, allergen and product groups for that kitchen. The exact coding is a local controlled specification, not a universal colour or abbreviation table.
- raw meat (СМ);
- raw fish (СР);
- raw vegetables (СО);
- cooked meat (ВМ);
- cooked fish (ВР);
- cooked vegetables (ВО);
- meat gastronomy (МГ);
- greens;
- pickled vegetables (КО);
- herring;
- bread (Х);
- fish gastronomy (РГ);
- raw poultry (СП);
- gastronomy (Г).

Apply the same identification and segregation logic to all tools in use, including employee-owned tools where permitted. The establishment must define and audit its own validated coding.

### Vegetable and egg processing containers
Use separate identified containers and a validated process flow for vegetable/greens processing and three containers for egg processing where those products are used and separate washing sinks are unavailable. Validate the arrangement against the current layout, process flow and official requirements before adopting.

### Cleaning inventory, waste and chemicals
The sanitation control system should ensure:
- each mop, bucket and cloth to be identifiable by zone (production, storage, auxiliary rooms, toilets);
- waste bins to be marked «ОТХОДЫ» or with a pictogram;
- any container into which a detergent/disinfectant is decanted from the supplier's original packaging to identify the product and purpose;
- no anonymous/unidentified chemical containers in production.

Do not infer chemical dilution, contact time or compatibility from a label abbreviation. Use the manufacturer's label and the restaurant's approved sanitation procedure.

### Delivery containers
For takeaway/delivery containers, the checklist lists product name, manufacture date, shelf life, storage conditions and allergen-related ingredient information. It also lists additional packaging/insert/receipt information in the source. Verify current consumer-information and technical-regulation requirements for the product and delivery format.

## 3. Practical control loop

For every relevant storage/marking audit:
1. Identify product, zone, process and date.
2. Record what is actually observed, with photo/document/source if permitted.
3. Check separation of raw, ready-to-eat, frozen, chilled, dry, wet, food and non-food items.
4. Check container closure and integrity.
5. Check label completeness against the approved current rule for that product.
6. Check board/knife/utensil identification and segregation.
7. Check cleaning inventory by zone.
8. Check chemical container identity and supplier instructions.
9. Record deviation, immediate containment, responsible role and verification.
10. Escalate suspected contamination, unsafe temperature control, unknown chemicals or allergen risk immediately.

## 4. Temperature and time values

The supplied materials do not provide a complete current temperature/time matrix for all products and processes. Do not invent one. Retrieve the product-specific approved standard or current official requirement before assessing a temperature deviation.

## 5. Legal and source cautions

The product-adjacency PDF contains historic fine figures and a reference to Article 6.6 of the Russian Administrative Offences Code. The figures and applicability are not validated here and must not be quoted as current penalties without checking the current official legal text.

The supplied materials identify a useful control framework; they do not constitute HACCP certification, a production-control programme, or proof of legal compliance.

## Stage 11 professional practice expansion — food-safety leadership

The following operating model combines procurement control, internal verification, food-safety governance and production-flow risk management. It is a DUMRAUF-authored framework; local roles, review cadence and control limits must be configured and verified.

### Food-safety working group
Identify a responsible leader and competent cross-functional participants from production, receiving/storage, service and maintenance as appropriate. The working cycle is: identify process and hazards → review control measures and ownership → define documented monitoring → handle deviations → verify corrective action → update training and procedures. Membership, meeting frequency, critical limits and authority are establishment-specific.

### Internal verification loop
Set audit scope and evidence criteria, use qualified independent-enough reviewers, record observations and objective evidence, classify deviations by actual risk, assign containment and root-cause investigation, document corrective action, verify effectiveness, and track repeated findings. An audit finding is not closed by writing “corrected”; verify that the problem no longer recurs.

### Receiving and design interface
Food safety starts with supplier approval, transport and delivery checks, traceability, stock separation and controlled movement into production. Review flows of raw material, ready-to-eat food, dirty dishes, waste and people when changing a kitchen layout. Avoid universal temperatures, storage durations or critical limits unless verified for the specific product, equipment and current applicable rules. For fire safety, maintain practical staff awareness of alarms, routes, equipment, incident reporting and assigned response roles; do not infer building compliance from a historical instruction.
