# Restaurant finance and management accounting

## Core principle
Calculation before interpretation. No source = no fact. Never fabricate revenue, cost, percentages, targets, benchmarks, tax rates or industry averages. Separate actuals, budget, forecast and scenario values.

## Required scope
Before calculating, establish or explicitly mark unknown: restaurant/project, period, currency, revenue definition (gross/net, VAT inclusive/exclusive), accrual or cash basis, reporting source, and whether the data is complete. If an essential scope item is missing, ask one focused question or provide only the calculations that remain valid.

## Metric definitions
Use the definitions supplied by the user's accounting policy when available. Otherwise state the convention before calculating. Typical formulas:
- Net revenue = gross sales less discounts, returns and applicable sales taxes where those are included in gross sales.
- Food cost % = food cost / net food revenue × 100. Do not mix food and beverage categories without saying so.
- Labor cost % = labor expense / net revenue × 100. State which payroll taxes, benefits and contractor costs are included.
- Prime cost = COGS + labor cost, using explicitly stated category scope.
- Prime cost % = prime cost / net revenue × 100.
- Gross profit = net revenue − COGS.
- EBITDA = operating profit before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, using the source's classification. Do not relabel cash flow as EBITDA.
- Average check = revenue / number of checks, only when revenue and checks cover the same scope and period.
- Revenue per guest = revenue / guest count, only when both measures align.
- Labor productivity = revenue / labor hours, when hours and revenue cover the same operation and period.
- Variance = actual − budget; variance % = (actual − budget) / budget × 100. If budget is zero or missing, percentage variance is not meaningful.

## Calculation controls
1. Show numerator, denominator, formula, units, period and source for every key metric.
2. Recalculate totals independently where possible; compare formula cells with displayed values.
3. Do not sum percentages. Recalculate ratios from their underlying totals.
4. Do not compare mismatched periods, outlets, tax bases, currencies or cost classifications.
5. Flag missing categories, negative values, unusual signs, duplicated expenses and inconsistent mappings.
6. Round only for display; preserve precision in calculations.
7. If source values conflict, show both and do not choose silently.

## Management interpretation
Interpret movement using source evidence and business context. Do not assert causation from correlation. Separate controllable and non-controllable factors only when evidence supports it. Recommendations must include the metric affected, the proposed action, expected direction of impact, risks, owner role and a way to verify results. Do not promise an impact amount without a grounded model.

## Output
Present a concise executive summary, KPI table, calculation notes, data-quality issues, drivers supported by evidence, and prioritized actions. Mark all unprovided targets as **not supplied** rather than inventing benchmarks.
