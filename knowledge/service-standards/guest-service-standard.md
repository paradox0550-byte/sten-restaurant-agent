# Guest Service Standard — Generalized STEN Edition

**Evidence class:** CORE (generalized from the supplied internal service standard)  
**Scope:** adaptable restaurant service framework; not a venue-specific SOP.

## 1. Service philosophy
The objective is not merely to complete transactions. It is to create a respectful, consistent experience that meets the guest's stated needs and gives them a reason to return. Food quality, service, atmosphere, reliability, and perceived value work together; one cannot reliably compensate for a serious failure in another.

## 2. Before service
- Arrive ready according to the approved local schedule.
- Check uniform, hygiene, tools, table readiness, cleanliness, reservations, menu availability, and current unavailable items.
- Attend the pre-shift briefing: expected demand, menu changes, allergens and dietary information, special events, staffing, zones, priorities, and known risks.
- Confirm who owns each zone and who is the escalation manager.
- Do not present an unavailable item as available. Do not guess ingredients, allergens, preparation times, or substitutions.

## 3. Arrival and seating
- Acknowledge arriving guests promptly, even if occupied; communicate that assistance is coming.
- Welcome in a natural, culturally appropriate way; avoid forced scripts.
- Confirm reservation details discreetly when applicable.
- Ask party size and seating preferences. Do not infer relationship, status, gender roles, spending capacity, or accessibility needs.
- Offer a realistic alternative if the preferred table is unavailable, including an honest wait estimate only when supported.
- Escort guests when appropriate and explain who will look after them.
- Respect mobility, hearing, visual, sensory, child-safety, and other access needs without drawing unnecessary attention.

## 4. First contact and menu orientation
- Introduce yourself and explain how to request assistance.
- Offer menus and relevant formats (large print, translated, digital) when available.
- Read the table's cues; do not interrupt an active conversation unnecessarily.
- Tell guests when you will return if they need time.
- Notice clear signals that a guest wants help, but do not stare or hover.

## 5. Discovering needs
Use a small number of open and clarifying questions:
- “What kind of meal are you in the mood for?”
- “Are there any ingredients or dietary requirements we should account for?”
- “Would you prefer something lighter or more substantial?”
- “Are you working to a particular time?”
Listen without interruption. Paraphrase important requirements and confirm details. Ask one question at a time and allow a pause.

## 6. Recommendations and ethical selling
- Recommend only items whose ingredients, preparation, availability, and price are known.
- Explain relevant taste, portion, preparation, and wait-time characteristics.
- Offer alternatives rather than steering every guest to the most expensive option.
- Cross-sell only when the addition is genuinely complementary and useful.
- Upsell only with transparent differences in quality, portion, ingredients, or experience.
- Do not use false scarcity, fabricated popularity, social pressure, hidden charges, or manipulative framing.
- Disclose allergens and dietary limitations according to the venue's approved procedure; never promise “allergen-free” without verified controls.

## 7. Order taking and confirmation
- Record the order accurately in the approved system.
- Clarify modifiers, cooking preferences, allergies, sharing, course timing, and beverage sequence when relevant.
- Repeat back material details and confirm the order.
- Communicate realistic timing based on current kitchen/bar information; update the guest if it changes.
- Never invent an ingredient or preparation detail.

## 8. During the meal
- Coordinate timing across courses and service stations.
- Deliver the correct items to the correct guests; identify dishes clearly.
- Use safe, practiced carrying and serving procedures.
- Observe without hovering. Check satisfaction at a natural moment after the first taste or when appropriate to the service style.
- Respond to requests promptly or acknowledge them and give a realistic update.
- Clear and reset only when appropriate; do not remove items a guest is still using without checking.

## 9. Bill and departure
- Present the bill discreetly and accurately.
- Explain charges or corrections transparently.
- Process payment only through approved procedures.
- Thank guests sincerely; do not make tipping assumptions or pressure guests.
- Invite a return naturally, without a scripted demand.
- Maintain attention and courtesy after payment until the guest leaves.

## 10. Complaint and service recovery
Use the sequence:
1. Listen without interruption.
2. Acknowledge the guest's experience; do not argue or blame.
3. Clarify the specific problem and desired outcome.
4. Apologize sincerely when service failed; do not use conditional or dismissive language.
5. Offer only remedies within delegated authority.
6. Escalate safety, allergy, injury, discrimination, threats, suspected contamination, or unresolved conflict immediately.
7. Confirm the agreed remedy and timing.
8. Follow up to ensure the issue was actually resolved.
9. Record the event, source, cause if verified, remedy, owner, and recurrence-prevention action.

A complaint is not closed merely because a remedy was offered. Completion and verification are separate states.

## 11. Etiquette and communication
- Use respectful forms of address; do not assume familiarity.
- Respect personal space and the guest's preferred interaction level.
- Keep voice, posture, gestures, and facial expression calm and attentive.
- Avoid personal phone use, staff-only conversations in guest areas, comments about guests, and visible frustration.
- Never discriminate based on age, gender, disability, nationality, appearance, language, family status, or perceived wealth.
- Adapt to cultural context without stereotyping. When uncertain, ask politely.

## 12. Personalization boundaries
Adapt to what the guest says and does in the current interaction. Do not diagnose personality types, emotional states, income, or identity from appearance, accent, clothing, or a single gesture. A guest's silence may mean many things; do not interpret it as consent or dissatisfaction without checking.

## 13. Service metrics
Use a balanced set: acknowledgement time; order accuracy; quoted vs actual wait; complaint incidence and recurrence; recovery time; guest feedback; accessibility issues; return behavior where lawfully measured; staff workload. Metrics require defined sources and denominators. Do not reward speed at the expense of safety, accuracy, or dignity.
